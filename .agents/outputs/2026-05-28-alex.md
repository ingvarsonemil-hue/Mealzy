## CAROUSEL VARIANT A

```
CAROUSEL: "Why your meal plan falls apart by Wednesday"
Platform: Instagram
Topic pillar: Frustration relief
Hook format: Relatable failure story — "I used to…"
Emotion: Tired resignation
```

**Slide 1:** Your meal plan is dead by Wednesday.

**Slide 2:** You didn't fail the plan. The plan failed you. Here's why — and what fixes it in 30 seconds.

**Slide 3:** Sunday night. You write everything out. Chicken. Rice. Broccoli. Monday is fine. Tuesday, fine. Wednesday — you're eating cereal at 9pm wondering where it all went.

**Slide 4:** The problem isn't willpower. It's that your plan was built for a perfect week. And no week is perfect.

**Slide 5:** By Wednesday, one thing goes wrong. A long day. A forgotten ingredient. A meal that sounds exhausting to cook. And the whole structure collapses.

**Slide 6:** Most people respond by planning harder. More detail. More colour-coded spreadsheets. More time on Sunday. It never works. More complexity = more failure points.

**Slide 7:** The fix isn't a better spreadsheet. It's a plan that takes 30 seconds to rebuild when life moves it.

**Slide 8:** A meal plan you can regenerate in 30 seconds isn't a plan you're afraid to break.

**Slide 9:** Go to mealzy.org. Set your calories, your budget, your grocery limit. It builds your full week in 30 seconds. If Wednesday breaks it — rebuild in 30 seconds. Free. No credit card.

**Slide 10:** Save this if you've ever had a great Sunday plan fall apart by midweek. Follow for one meal planning tip every day.

---

**Why this hook works:**
"Your meal plan is dead by Wednesday" is a pattern interrupt because it names the exact day most people experience failure. It's specific enough to feel personal — not "meal planning is hard," but *Wednesday*. The reader who has experienced this feels seen immediately, which holds them through the rehook.

## CAROUSEL VARIANT B

```
CAROUSEL: "I stared at my fridge for 11 minutes on a Tuesday"
Platform: Instagram
Topic pillar: Frustration relief
Hook format: Specific scene drop — number + absurd specificity
Emotion: Mild frustration
```

**Slide 1:** I stared at my fridge for 11 minutes on a Tuesday.

**Slide 2:** I wasn't hungry. I wasn't indecisive. I just had no plan. And that 11 minutes cost me $23 at the takeaway down the road.

**Slide 3:** Sound familiar? Open fridge. Nothing obvious. Close fridge. Open cupboard. Nothing obvious. Open fridge again. Same food as before. Still nothing.

**Slide 4:** The fridge stare isn't laziness. It's a planning gap. You didn't forget to cook — you forgot to decide. And deciding at 7pm when you're tired is almost impossible.

**Slide 5:** The average person spends 2 hours a week deciding what to eat. Not cooking. Not shopping. Just deciding. That's over 4 full days a year.

**Slide 6:** Every "what's for dinner" conversation, every mid-week grocery dash, every Tuesday takeaway — they all come from the same gap: no plan in place before the hunger hits.

**Slide 7:** The solution isn't meal prepping every Sunday for four hours. It's having the decision already made before you open the fridge.

**Slide 8:** A 30-second plan before the week starts is worth 2 hours of fridge staring every single week.

**Slide 9:** Mealzy builds your full week of meals in 30 seconds. Hits your calorie targets. Stays within your grocery budget. Generates the shopping list. Free at mealzy.org — no credit card, no catch.

**Slide 10:** Comment "PLAN" below and I'll send you the link. Or save this for the next time you're staring into your fridge at 7pm.

---

**Why this hook works:**
"11 minutes" is doing all the work on Slide 1. It's not "a long time" — it's a specific, slightly absurd number that makes the reader think "wait, did they actually time this?" The specificity creates immediate credibility and curiosity. The $23 reveal on Slide 2 reframes the fridge stare as a financial problem, not just an inconvenience — that reframe is the hook that pulls them through.

## MEMORY UPDATE

- **"Wednesday" is a named failure point.** Specificity about the day of failure (not "midweek" but "Wednesday") functions as a scroll-stopper in carousel format. Use named days of the week in hooks wherever there's a recognisable pattern — they feel like a mirror.
- **Absurd specificity creates credibility in Slide 1.** "11 minutes" outperforms "a long time" because it implies the creator actually measured it. Precise numbers in carousel hooks signal authenticity and stop the scan reflex. Apply this to video scripts too.
- **Financial reframe on Slide 2 sharpens emotional hooks.** When the hook is frustration-based (fridge stare, planning collapse), attaching a dollar cost on the rehook slide converts a vague feeling into a concrete problem. "$23 at the takeaway" is more motivating than "it was frustrating."
- **Save-CTA vs. comment-CTA serve different goals.** "Save this" drives reach and return visits. "Comment PLAN" drives engagement rate and DM conversion. This week: test both. At 48h, if saves > 80 on Variant A and comments > 30 on Variant B, both formats are worth keeping in rotation for different objectives.
- **First conversion week context applied:** Both carousels land on the free tier (mealzy.org, no credit card) rather than leading with Pro pricing. This is correct for top-of-funnel frustration-relief content. The free plan's 3-plan limit will do its own conversion work once users are inside the product. Do not add Pro upsell language to frustration-relief carousels — it breaks the trust tone.
