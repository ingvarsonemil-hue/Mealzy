No support tickets exist yet. Based on product structure and standard patterns for freemium meal planners, the three themes most likely to dominate once volume arrives, in order:

**1. Free tier plan limit ("I only get 3 plans?")**
Users will hit the wall mid-month and not understand why they can't generate another plan. **2. Account or login issues**
JWT cookie auth on a free-tier Render instance means sessions may drop unexpectedly. Users who lose their session before saving their first plan are gone. First contact with support will often be "I made a plan and now it's gone."

**3. What's included in Pro vs. Free**
PDF export, Mia coach, advanced macros — the distinction is not always obvious to a user mid-flow. Expect confusion about why certain features are grayed out or missing. A short, scannable comparison page or tooltip set would reduce this ticket volume before it starts.

### Fix Recommendation for Quinn

**Priority: Profile completion flow audit — this is now three weeks overdue.**

Specifically, Quinn needs to answer two questions and make a call on each:

1. Is profile completion required before a user can generate their first plan, or can they skip it? If skippable, the default behavior should nudge strongly toward completion — a partial profile produces a worse plan, and a worse first plan kills D1 retention.

2. How many steps or fields does the profile page have right now? If it takes more than 60–90 seconds to complete, it needs to be trimmed or split into a short required set (dietary restrictions, goal, household size) and an optional extended set that can be filled later.

The fix does not need to be perfect before launch. It needs to be good enough that a first-time visitor can go from signup to first generated plan in under three minutes without confusion or dead ends.

### Email Improvement for Jordan

**Priority: Confirm platform and begin 3-email onboarding sequence — this is now three weeks overdue.**

If no email platform is in place yet, the recommendation remains Resend or Loops — both are simple to integrate with a Node/Express stack, both have free tiers that cover early-stage volume, and both can be wired to Mealzy's existing Turso user table with minimal backend work.

The sequence to build first, in order of impact:

**Email 1 — Send immediately after signup**
Subject line option: "Your first meal plan is ready to build"
Purpose: Get the user back into the app and through their first plan generation within 24 hours. This is the single highest-leverage email in the entire funnel. A strong D1 open and click here directly moves D1 retention.
Content: One sentence of welcome, one sentence on what Mealzy does, one clear CTA button — "Build your first plan" linking to mealzy.org/dashboard.html. No feature list, no long copy.

**Email 2 — Send on Day 3 if no plan has been generated**
Subject line option: "Takes about two minutes — here's how"
Purpose: Recover users who signed up but never made a plan. Acknowledge the barrier is usually just not knowing where to start. Show one concrete example of what a plan looks like. Single CTA.

**Email 3 — Send on Day 6, triggered when user hits 2 of 3 free plans used**
Subject line option: "You have one free plan left this month"
Purpose: Soft Pro upgrade prompt timed to when the user has already seen value. Not a hard sell. One line on what Pro adds (unlimited plans, PDF export, Mia). Link to mealzy.org/upgrade.html.
