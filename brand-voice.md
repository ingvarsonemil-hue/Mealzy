# Mealzy brand voice
Last updated: 2026-05-20

This is the source of truth for how Mealzy sounds and how Mealzy video ads are built. Every piece of content — TikTok, Reels, Shorts, blog, email, landing page, push notification — should be created from this document. Video ads are structured explicitly around the Blotato viral scoring rubric (100 points total). The target score for every Mealzy video is 80+/100.

---

## Persona

Mealzy is the nutritionist friend who actually gets you. Warm, calm, optimistic. Knowledgeable but not preachy. Confident but not pushy. Modern but not bro-y.

We speak as "we" (the Mealzy team) to "you" (one real person). Mealzy itself shows up as a named third character that does cool things — "Mealzy spits out a plan," "Mealzy hits your macros." You and Mealzy share top billing as the heroes of the story. You bring the goals; Mealzy does the work.

The core tension: hooks open like a confident stranger grabbing your attention. The body and payoff land like a friend explaining the thing. Never sell in a way that makes the viewer feel sold to.

---

## Voice attributes

**Warm.** We talk to a person, not a market. Friendly without being performative. Never moralize about food or bodies.

**Calm.** No exclamation pile-ons. No urgency manipulation. No fake scarcity. Quiet confidence reads louder than hype. Calm is not flat — there is always a specific emotion underneath: mild frustration, quiet relief, dry amusement. Pick one and play it.

**Optimistic.** Eating well is doable, not a moral test. Celebrate progress without overdoing the "you got this!!!" energy.

**Direct.** Short sentences. Real verbs. Name the problem before the solution. Specific numbers always.

**Genuinely honest.** "Genuinely" is in. "Honestly" works. Plain-spoken sincerity belongs.

---

## Voice we are NOT

- Preachy or moralizing about food
- Try-hard, cringe, or chasing meme energy
- Overly technical (no jargon dumps)
- Pushy or salesy
- Snarky or mean
- Bro-y, gym-bro, or diet-culture-coded
- Granola, wellness-influencer, or woo-woo

---

## Style rules

- Short. Punchy. Sentences are fine.
- Real verbs: spits out, builds, handles, hits, fixes, plans, plates up.
- Specific numbers always: "30 seconds," "$60 a month," "7 meals," "±50 calories," "28 seconds."
- Active voice always.
- Proper capitalization. Not all-lowercase. Not ALL CAPS for emphasis.
- One exclamation point max per piece. Usually zero.
- No emojis anywhere — captions, overlays, copy, push notifications. Zero.
- Question hooks can use double ?? ("ever stared at your fridge at 7pm??").

---

## Words and phrases to use

- Genuinely / honestly / actually
- Plate up / plan / build / hit / handle / sort out / spits out
- "Free at mealzy.org"
- "In about 30 seconds" / "in 2 minutes" / "in 28 seconds"
- "Your goals" / "your week" / "your fridge" / "your budget"

---

## Banned words and phrases

- Level up / game-changer / transform your life / crush your goals
- Clean eating / guilt-free / cheat day / superfood / detox
- Best-kept secret / "You won't believe…" / "What if I told you…"
- "Hack" as a noun / "Friend" / "Babes" / "queen" / "girlie" / "fam"
- Anything implying food is morally "good" or "bad"
- WARNING: / DEAD / NOW / HOURS (all-caps for shock value)
- Fake stats: "98% of people," "99% of people," "ONE huge mistake"
- "The secret the food industry doesn't want you to know"
- "Limited time only" (unless literally true)
- "Don't miss out!" / "Last chance!" / "Click the link in bio now!!!"

---

## How to handle weight and bodies

Mention weight loss naturally alongside other goals — muscle, energy, time saved, money saved. Never as the whole frame.

Do: use neutral language ("hit your goals," "fit your targets"), cite real attributed results, talk macros and calories factually.

Don't: promise specific weight loss in specific timeframes, use before/after framing without context, say "thin" / "skinny" / "shredded" / "snatched" as goals, talk to the reader's body like it's a problem.

# VIDEO ADS — BUILT TO SCORE

The Blotato viral scoring rubric is 100 points split across 5 dimensions. Every Mealzy video ad must be built to hit each dimension explicitly. This section maps the rubric and tells you exactly what to do for each one.

---

## DIMENSION 1 — Scroll-Stopper (35 points)

**What Blotato scores:** The opening frame at 0:00–0:01. Can this single still image stop a thumb mid-scroll with the sound off?

This is the highest-weighted single dimension in the rubric. It has been the consistent failure point across every Mealzy ad tested (scores: 6/35, 8/35, 10/35, 13/35, 15/35). The fix is the same every time: bold on-screen text + a disruptive physical action.

### Rule 1 — Bold on-screen text at 0:00. Non-negotiable.

Text must appear before the creator opens their mouth. High contrast. One line. Under 8 words. This is the hook for every muted viewer and the primary scroll-stop signal for the algorithm.

**Proven on-screen text lines for Mealzy:**
- `You're wasting 2 hours every Sunday.`
- `I cut my grocery bill by $60.`
- `30 seconds. Whole week planned.`
- `Meal planning binders are dead.`
- `This took me 28 seconds.`
- `Stop planning meals by hand.`

Without this text, the scroll-stopper score is capped regardless of how good everything else is. This has been confirmed in every ad tested.

### Rule 2 — The opening action must be visually unexpected.

The first 0.5 seconds must contain a physical action that is not generic. An attractive person standing in a nice kitchen scores 13–15/35. An attractive person in a nice kitchen doing something unexpected scores higher.

**Actions that work:**
- Throwing a binder / stack of printed meal plans off the counter
- Phone or notebook slamming down with frustration
- Pulling out earbuds and turning to camera mid-frustration
- Ripping up a grocery list
- Pushing away a laptop / spreadsheet
- Holding up a phone showing a chaotic overflowing notes app

**Actions that don't work (confirmed failures):**
- Opening a fridge with back to camera
- Standing at a counter in normal lighting
- Sitting at a desk with a neutral expression
- Any scene that could be a stock photo

### Rule 3 — Face camera from frame one.

Direct eye contact in the first frame signals confidence. Back to camera = no engagement signal, score capped.

### The thumbnail test

Take a single still frame from 0:00–0:01. Ask honestly: would someone stop scrolling for this image alone? If no, change the opening action before shooting.

---

## DIMENSION 2 — Problem/Benefit Statement (30 points)

**What Blotato scores:** What the creator says in the first 3 seconds. Does it name a specific problem and deliver a concrete benefit — or at least a strong tension — inside the hook?

### Rule 1 — Problem AND payoff in the first sentence.

Not problem in sentence one, payoff in sentence two. Both in the same breath.

| Weak (scores ~17/30) | Strong (scores ~26/30) |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| "This was me every single night until I found Mealzy." | "I used to spend 90 minutes on meal planning. Now it takes 30 seconds." |
| "I don't usually recommend apps. I'm recommending this one." | "I don't usually recommend apps. This one cut my meal prep from 2 hours to 30 seconds." |
| "I found something that changed how I do meal prep." | "I cut my grocery bill by $60 a month without eating worse." |

### Rule 2 — Always use a specific number.

Numbers are the difference between a vague claim and a credible one. Every hook must contain at least one real number: a time, a dollar amount, a day count.

Good numbers for Mealzy:
- 30 seconds / 28 seconds / 2 minutes
- 2 hours (the time being saved)
- $60 a month / $90 a week / $180 a year
- 7 days / one week / a full week
- 1,800 calories / your exact calorie target

### Rule 3 — Write pain from inside the experience, not outside it.

Blotato scores emotion as a separate dimension (see below). But the spoken hook is where the emotional framing starts.

| Outside the experience (weaker) | Inside the experience (stronger) |
|--------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| "Tracking macros in a spreadsheet takes time." | "I was spending more time logging food than actually working out." |
| "Meal planning every Sunday is tedious." | "I used to lose my whole Sunday afternoon just figuring out what to eat." |
| "It's hard to stay on budget at the grocery store." | "I'd buy a load of stuff and half of it would go bad because nothing went together." |

### Rule 4 — No vague before/after as the opening line.

"This was me until I found Mealzy" scored 17/30. It creates recognition but no tension. If you use before/after framing, the specific number must be in the same sentence.

### Spoken hook formats — ranked by tested performance

**Tier 1 — Use these first.**

| Format | Template | Target score |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| Time waste | "You're wasting [X hours] every [time period] on [specific task]." | 25–28/30 |
| Problem + payoff combined | "I used to spend [X time] on [task]. Now it takes [Y seconds]." | 24–27/30 |
| Specific result first | "I cut [specific thing] by [$X] without [negative trade-off]." | 24–26/30 |
| Direct accusation | "If you're still [doing old way], you're choosing to make your life harder." | 22–25/30 |
| Category kill | "[Old thing] is dead. Here's what replaced mine." | 22–25/30 |
| Scene drop with payoff | "[Scene]. [Scene]. I fixed that in [X seconds]." | 22–24/30 |

**Tier 2 — Use only with a number in the same sentence.**

| Format | Safe version | Risky version |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Earned curiosity | "I don't usually recommend apps. This one cut my meal prep from 2 hours to 30 seconds." | "I don't usually recommend apps. I'm recommending this one." — scored 6/30 |

---

## DIMENSION 3 — Sound Test (5 points)

**What Blotato scores:** Does the hook work with the sound completely off?

This is the easiest 5 points in the rubric. On-screen text at 0:00 handles it automatically. If Dimension 1 Rule 1 is followed — bold text on screen before the creator speaks — this dimension scores full marks.

No additional work needed if the on-screen text line is in place.

---

## DIMENSION 4 — Info Density (15 points)

**What Blotato scores:** How much specific, useful information does the video contain? Are there real numbers, real claims, real product details?

Current average across Mealzy ads tested: 10–12/15. This dimension is already performing well. The guidance here is to maintain it, not overload it.

### Rules for info density

- One clear claim per sentence. Never two.
- Mention the specific number at least once in the bridge: 30 seconds, the weekly budget, the calorie target.
- Show the app on screen during the bridge — the interface counts as information.
- Cover 2–3 features max. More than that dilutes each claim.
- The grocery list is a high-value claim — always mention it. It is a concrete, tangible output that viewers understand immediately.

**The core Mealzy value prop in order of impact:**
1. Builds a full week of meals in 30 seconds
2. Hits your calorie / macro targets
3. Stays within your grocery budget
4. Generates the grocery list automatically
5. Free — no credit card

Not all five need to be in every video. Pick the 2–3 that match the hook's angle.

---

## DIMENSION 5 — Emotions (15 points)

**What Blotato scores:** Does the video create an emotional response? Does the viewer feel something — recognition, relief, frustration, curiosity?

Current average across Mealzy ads tested: 8–9/15. This is the second weakest dimension after the visual hook.

### The fix: write from inside the feeling

The scripts are informative. They are not emotional. The gap is not drama — it is specificity of feeling.

**How to move from fact to feeling:**

| Fact (scores 8–9/15 emotions) | Feeling (targets 12–13/15 emotions) |
|-------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| "Meal planning takes a long time." | "I'd lose my whole Sunday to it and still feel behind by Monday." |
| "It's hard to stay on budget." | "I'd spend $120 at the shop and still have nothing to make for dinner." |
| "Tracking macros in a spreadsheet is tedious." | "I was logging food for 40 minutes a day. It was supposed to help. It just made me resent eating." |
| "It's hard to know what to cook at 7pm." | "I'd open the fridge, stare at it, close it, open it again. Every single night." |

### Delivery notes for emotion score

- Pick one specific emotion for the hook: mild frustration, quiet relief, dry amusement, or tired resignation. Play it consistently through the first 5 seconds.
- Do not smile during the problem statement. Smile after the solution lands.
- Deadpan is fine. Flat is not. The difference is a specific emotion underneath the calm.
- The relief in the payoff should feel earned — the heavier the problem, the more the solution lands.

---

## VIDEO STRUCTURE — complete template

Every Mealzy video ad follows this structure. Times are targets, not hard limits.

```
[0:00–0:01] ON-SCREEN TEXT — one line, bold, high contrast, 8 words max
            Appears before the creator speaks. No exceptions.

[0:00–0:02] OPENING ACTION — unexpected physical action
            Something disruptive. Not "stands in kitchen."

[0:00–0:03] SPOKEN HOOK — problem + payoff in the same sentence
            Specific number required. No greetings. No setup. Direct eye contact.

[0:03–0:18] BRIDGE — 2–3 claims, one per sentence
            Show the app. Use specific numbers. Follow the core value prop order.

[0:18–0:25] PAYOFF + CTA — one honest personal result, then the CTA
            "Free at mealzy.org." Never two CTAs. Pause 0.5s before the CTA.
```

Total length: 20–30 seconds optimal. Under 15 feels rushed. Over 35 loses retention.

---

## PRE-SHOOT CHECKLIST

Before any video is shot or generated, confirm all five items. If any are missing, do not proceed.

- [ ] **On-screen text written** — exact words, under 8 words, high contrast, appears at 0:00
- [ ] **Opening action defined** — specific unexpected physical action described in one sentence
- [ ] **Spoken hook written verbatim** — first sentence only, contains a specific number, problem + payoff in same breath
- [ ] **2–3 bridge claims selected** — from the core value prop list, matched to the hook angle
- [ ] **Emotion identified** — one specific feeling for the creator to play during the hook

## When in doubt

Read it out loud. If it sounds like a wellness influencer, rewrite it. If it sounds like a friend who happens to know nutrition, ship it.

For video hooks: look at the first frame as a still image. Ask "would I stop scrolling for this?" If no — new opening action. Then check: is there bold text on screen before a word is spoken? If no — add it. Then check: does the first sentence contain a specific number and a payoff? If no — rewrite the hook.
