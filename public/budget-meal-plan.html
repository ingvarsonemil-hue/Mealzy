How to eat well on $50 a week

The $50/week grocery budget is achievable for most people who are cooking at home — but it requires a plan. The biggest enemy of budget cooking is impulse decisions: buying what looks good at the shop without knowing what you'll actually use it for.

Here's what makes the difference:

  • Plan before you shop. Know exactly what you're eating each day before you leave the house. A 30-minute meal plan on Sunday saves £20–£30 in wasted food and impulse buying during the week.
  • Anchor your week on cheap proteins. Eggs, lentils, canned tuna, canned chickpeas, chicken thighs (not breasts), and tofu are the cheapest protein sources per gram. Build meals around them.
  • Batch cook twice a week. Cook a large pot of lentil soup or pasta sauce on Sunday and Wednesday. One cook, three meals.
  • Buy store-brand basics. Oats, rice, pasta, lentils, canned tomatoes, and canned beans — always buy store brand. The quality is identical; the price is 40% less.
  • Avoid pre-cut, pre-washed, and pre-marinated. A whole chicken costs a third of a pack of pre-cut pieces. A head of broccoli costs 30% less than pre-cut florets.
  • Use your freezer. Bread, bananas turning brown, leftover rice — freeze them. Reduces waste, extends the $50 further.

Common questions about budget meal planning

Is $50/week realistic for one person?

Yes — $50/week for one person is very achievable if you cook at home and plan your meals. The key is anchoring your plan on cheap protein sources (eggs, lentils, canned fish, chicken thighs) and minimising processed or pre-prepared food. Mealzy can generate a $50 plan around any calorie target or dietary preference.

Can I set a different budget?

Yes. Mealzy accepts any weekly budget from $20 to $500. Set your budget in your profile and every generated plan stays within that amount. The grocery list shows the total estimated cost so you know before you shop.

Does it work for couples or families?

Yes. Set your budget to cover the household (e.g. $100/week for two people) and Mealzy scales the ingredient amounts accordingly. The Family plan supports up to 4 individual profiles each with separate calorie goals and dietary requirements — with a single combined grocery list for one shop.
